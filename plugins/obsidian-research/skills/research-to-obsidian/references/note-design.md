# Editorial note design

Make the note useful as a long-term reference, not a transcript of search results. Start with a precise title and a short deck explaining scope and date. Follow with an abstract callout that states the answer, a compact navigation table for long notes, and clearly named sections. Use tables for comparisons, timelines, or terminology; use prose for causal reasoning. Keep paragraphs short, but preserve enough detail, data, caveats, and counterevidence to make the note information-rich.

Place a cover image only if it is relevant, high-resolution, and permitted for reuse. Use at most one cover image and a small number of purposeful figures where they explain something the text cannot. For every image, write an alt description and a caption with creator, source URL, and license. Check that the local Obsidian embed resolves to a saved file. Avoid decorative stock imagery that adds no information.

Use consistent hierarchy: one H1, H2 for major topics, H3 for subdivisions. Use callouts sparingly for takeaway, caveat, and open question. Avoid a wall of callouts, emoji decoration, and tables too wide for mobile. The bundled CSS styles only notes carrying `cssclasses: [obsidian-research]`, but the Markdown must remain readable without it.

Separate three kinds of text visibly:

1. **Source-backed findings:** cite the source at the point of the claim. Distinguish direct evidence from an author's interpretation.
2. **Assistant analysis:** explain the inference, its assumptions, and the strongest objection; label it as the assistant's view.
3. **Open questions:** list what could change the conclusion or what could not be verified.

For translated sources, prepare a terminology table with original expression, chosen translation, and the reason for the choice when a term is non-obvious. Check every material number, date, unit, name, negation, and qualifier against the original. Do not silently smooth over ambiguity. Keep short original-language phrases where they are necessary to show the basis of a disputed translation.

Before saving, verify the title, dates, source links, image paths, image rights and credits, citations for key claims, and that the central answer appears early. After saving, read the Markdown back and inspect it in Obsidian Reading View when the app is available. Repair visible line wrapping, broken embeds, or callout formatting.
